1. What this is

A family tree you can build, explore, and pass around. You add people, say how they're related, and it draws the tree for you automatically.

There are two ways you might be using it:

VersionWhat it means for you
A web link
(e.g. a github.io address)		 Opens in any browser. You can explore and make changes, but your changes stay on your own device. Think of it as your personal copy.
A file
(something like family-tree.html)		 Someone emailed or messaged it to you. Double-click to open. Works with no internet after the first load, and you can send an updated copy onwards.

Both look and work identically. The rest of this guide applies to either.

2. Opening it

If you were given a link

Click it. That's it — nothing to install, no account, no password.

If you were given a file

  1. Save the file somewhere you'll find it again — your Desktop or Documents folder.
  2. Double-click it. It opens in your usual web browser.
  3. If it opens in a text editor showing gibberish instead, right-click the file, choose Open with, and pick Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox.

3. A quick tour of the screen

Across the top you'll find these buttons:

ButtonWhat it does
Generations Web Switches between the two ways of drawing the tree. See section 6.
+ Add person Adds a new family member.
Save / Share file Downloads a copy of the tree with your changes included, ready to pass on.
JSON Downloads the raw data. Only useful if you want to do something technical with it — ignore otherwise.
Reset Throws away your changes and goes back to how the tree was when you received it.

Just below are three tabs:

  • Tree — the picture of your family.
  • People — a plain list of everyone, where you edit or delete them.
  • Relationships — where you say who is married to whom, and who is whose child.

Underneath the tree there's a colour key: blue for male, pink for female, purple for other, grey if not specified. A dark ring around someone means they've passed away. A gold dashed line means a marriage.

4. Adding your first people

  1. Click + Add person.
  2. Fill in what you know. Only the first name is required — everything else is optional, and you can always come back and add more later.
  3. Click Save.

The form is split into three sections so it's not overwhelming:

SectionWhat goes in it
BasicsFirst and last name, nickname, gender, and a link to a photo.
Life datesDate and place of birth; date and place of death if they've passed away.
Life detailsOccupation, education, a fun fact, and free-form notes.
Tip: start with yourself Add yourself first, then your parents, then their parents. Working upwards and outwards from someone you know well is much easier than trying to start at the top.

Editing or removing someone

Go to the People tab and click Edit next to their name. Delete removes them along with all their connections — you'll be asked to confirm first.

5. Connecting people together

Adding people puts them on the chart, but they'll float separately until you say how they're related. That's the Relationships tab.

  1. Open the Relationships tab.
  2. Pick the first person from the Person A dropdown.
  3. Choose is parent of or is married to.
  4. Pick the second person from Person B.
  5. Click Link. The tree redraws straight away.
Read it as a sentence The dropdowns are designed to read left to right like English: "Peter is parent of Ella". If that sentence is true, the link is right. Order matters for parents — getting it backwards puts the child above the parent.

Both parents

Add each parent separately: once for the mother, once for the father. The tree will draw connecting lines from both down to the child.

Removing a connection

Every link is listed underneath the form with a Remove button. Removing a link doesn't delete anybody — it just unhooks them.

6. The two tree views

Generations — the traditional chart

Each generation sits on its own row: grandparents at the top, then children, then grandchildren. Best for seeing the shape of the family at a glance, and the one to use when printing.

Web — the floating view

Everyone floats freely, connected by lines, and you can drag people around. Useful for untangling complicated or sprawling families.

Getting around

  • Zoom: scroll your mouse wheel, or pinch on a touchscreen.
  • Move around: click an empty area and drag.
  • See someone's details: click their circle. A panel appears with everything recorded about them. Close it with the ×.
  • Rearrange (Web view only): drag a person to move them.
Lost the tree off-screen? Switch to the other view and back again. It re-centres and re-fits everything automatically.

7. Saving and sharing your changes

This is the most important section, so read it carefully.

Your changes are remembered automatically in your own browser, so closing the tab won't lose your work. But that copy only exists on your device. Nobody else can see it, and it would be lost if you cleared your browser data or switched computers.

To keep your work properly, or send it to anyone else Click Save / Share file. That downloads a brand new copy of the tree with all your additions baked in. That downloaded file is the thing to keep safe and pass on.

How to share it onwards

  1. Click Save / Share file.
  2. Find the downloaded file (usually your Downloads folder, named something like our-family-tree-2026-05-14.html).
  3. Email it, message it, or drop it in a shared folder. The person receiving it double-clicks to open, and can carry on adding to it themselves.
The one real limitation Everyone works on their own copy. If you and your cousin both add people at the same time, those additions live in two separate files and won't merge themselves. For a family project, it works best if one person keeps the master copy — everyone sends their additions to them, and they send out the updated version.

Printing it

Switch to Generations view, arrange the tree how you'd like it, then press Ctrl+P (or Cmd+P on a Mac). Choosing landscape usually gives a better result. To save a PDF, pick "Save as PDF" as the destination.

8. Using it on a phone

It works on phones and tablets. The tree is smaller, so pinch to zoom and drag to move around. Tapping a person brings up their details across most of the screen.

Adding people works fine on a phone, but if you're entering a lot of relatives in one sitting, a computer with a keyboard will be far less tedious.

9. Common questions & troubleshooting

I added someone but they're not attached to anyone

That's expected — new people start unconnected. Go to the Relationships tab and link them up (section 5).

Someone's on the wrong row

Rows are worked out from the relationships, not entered by hand. If someone's in the wrong generation, a parent link is probably backwards or missing. Check the Relationships list and confirm each one reads correctly as a sentence.

The page is blank, or the tree won't appear

The tree drawing is fetched from the internet the first time you open the file, so you need to be online for that first load. If you're offline, connect and refresh. If it's still blank, try a different browser — Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all work.

I made a mess. Can I start over?

Yes — click Reset. It discards your changes and restores the tree to how it was when you received it. Anything you didn't download with Save / Share file will be gone, so save first if you're unsure.

My changes vanished

Most likely causes: you cleared your browsing data, you're using private/incognito mode (which forgets everything on close), or you've opened a different copy of the file. This is exactly why downloading via Save / Share file matters — that file is the permanent record.

Can two of us edit the same tree at the same time?

Not simultaneously, no. See the note in section 7 — nominate one person to hold the master copy.

How do I add a photo?

The photo field takes a web address (a link to an image already online), not a file from your computer. Paste the image's URL into the Photo URL box.

Is there a limit on how many people I can add?

Nothing you'll hit in practice. Very large trees get wide, so use zoom and drag to navigate, or switch to Web view for a more compact shape.

10. A note on privacy

Family trees hold exactly the information used to verify identity — full names, dates of birth, birthplaces, mothers' maiden names. Worth a moment's thought:

  • If you're viewing this at a public web address, treat anything in it as visible to the world, including search engines.
  • Prefer sending the file directly to relatives over publishing a public link containing real details.
  • Think twice before recording living people's full birth dates and birthplaces in anything you'll share widely.
  • There's deliberately nowhere to record phone numbers, email addresses or home addresses — that's intentional, since these files get passed around.
  • Ask before adding other people's details, especially children's.
